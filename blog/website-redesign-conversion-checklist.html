The 12-Point Conversion Checklist for Your Next Website Redesign

Redesigning your website? This 12-point checklist covers the conversion, speed and SEO essentials that separate a pretty site from a profitable one.

J
JeevaFounder, Digital Jeeva360 — AI-Powered Digital Marketer & Automation Specialist

A website redesign is one of the highest-leverage investments a business can make — and one of the easiest to get wrong. We've audited hundreds of sites, including many fresh redesigns that looked beautiful and converted worse than what they replaced. This checklist is how you avoid joining them.

Above the fold

1. A headline that passes the five-second test. A stranger should know what you do, who it's for, and why you're different within five seconds. "Welcome to our website" fails. So does clever wordplay that requires context. Clarity beats cleverness, every time.

2. One primary call to action, visible without scrolling. Decide the single action you want most — book a call, get a quote, start a trial — and make it the most visually prominent element on the page. Everything else is secondary.

3. Proof within the first screen. Review stars, client logos, a results statistic — something that signals "real businesses trust us" before the visitor invests any scroll.

Trust and persuasion

4. Specific social proof, not adjectives. "Increased bookings 3.2x in 90 days" persuades; "great service, highly recommend" wallpaper does not. Use testimonials with names, faces, numbers and context.

5. Answer objections where they occur. Price concerns belong near pricing, risk concerns near the CTA (guarantees, "no lock-in contracts"), capability concerns near case studies. An FAQ section is your objection-handling safety net.

6. Forms that respect effort. Every field you add cuts completion. Ask for what you need to respond usefully — usually name, contact and one qualifying question — and collect the rest on the call.

Performance and SEO (where redesigns go to die)

7. Sub-2.5-second loads on mobile. Test on a real phone over 4G, not your office fibre. Every second of delay costs roughly 7–20% of conversions, and Core Web Vitals affect rankings sitewide.

8. A complete redirect map before launch. Every old URL must 301-redirect to its closest new equivalent. Skipping this is the single most common way redesigns destroy years of SEO equity overnight.

9. Preserve (and improve) on-page SEO. Title tags, headings, internal links and schema markup should be migrated deliberately, not left for the new template's defaults.

10. Mobile-first, genuinely. Over 60% of your traffic is on a phone. Design and test for the phone first, then enhance for desktop — not the reverse.

After launch

11. Analytics and conversion tracking from day one. If you can't measure enquiries, calls and their sources, you can't improve them. Set up GA4 events and call tracking before launch, not "eventually".

12. Treat launch as the starting line. The best-performing sites are iterated: watch session recordings, review form drop-offs, A/B test the headline. A site that improves 5% monthly doubles its output in 15 months.

A redesign isn't a design project with some marketing attached. It's a revenue project that happens to involve design.

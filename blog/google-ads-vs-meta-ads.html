Google Ads vs Meta Ads: Where Should Your Budget Actually Go?

An honest comparison of Google Ads and Meta Ads — intent vs attention, cost dynamics, and a simple framework for splitting budget by business type.

J
JeevaFounder, Digital Jeeva360 — AI-Powered Digital Marketer & Automation Specialist

It's the most common question we get from businesses starting with paid ads: Google or Meta? The honest answer is that they do fundamentally different jobs, and the right split depends on how your customers buy — not on which platform is fashionable this quarter.

The core difference: intent vs attention

Google Ads captures existing demand. Someone types "emergency electrician Manchester" because they need an electrician, in Manchester, now. You're bidding to be the answer at the exact moment of need. Intent is the highest it will ever be — and you pay a premium for it.

Meta Ads creates demand. Nobody scrolls Instagram looking for your product. But with sharp creative and targeting, you can put a compelling offer in front of exactly the kind of person who buys it — interrupting them into interest. Clicks cost less, but they arrive colder.

Where Google wins

  • Urgent, high-intent services: repairs, legal, medical, trades. When the water heater bursts, nobody's waiting to be inspired by a Reel.
  • Considered purchases people research: "best CRM for small business", "invisalign cost" — searches that signal a buying process underway.
  • Provable ROI, quickly: with proper tracking, you know your cost per lead by keyword within weeks.

Where Meta wins

  • Visual, impulse-friendly products: fashion, beauty, home, food, gifts — anything that sells on sight.
  • New or unfamiliar offers: if nobody searches for your category yet, there's no demand to capture — you have to create it.
  • Audience building and retargeting: Meta's ability to warm cold audiences with video and then retarget engaged viewers is unmatched for the price.

The costs, honestly

Google's cost per click runs higher — often $3–$15 for services, and far more in legal or finance — but conversion rates are strong because intent is strong. Meta's clicks might cost $0.50–$3, but expect lower conversion rates and a longer path to purchase. Comparing platforms on CPC alone is the classic beginner's mistake; cost per acquisition is the only number that matters.

A simple allocation framework

Business typeSuggested starting splitLogic
Local services (trades, clinics, legal)80% Google / 20% MetaCapture urgent demand first; Meta for brand and retargeting
E-commerce30% Google / 70% MetaMeta drives discovery; Google Shopping + Brand closes searchers
B2B / SaaS60% Google / 40% MetaSearch captures active evaluations; Meta nurtures the long cycle
New product category15% Google / 85% MetaNo search volume yet — demand must be created

The multiplier most businesses miss

The platforms aren't competitors — they're stages of the same funnel. Meta introduces, Google closes, retargeting reminds.

A customer sees your Meta ad on Tuesday, googles your brand on Thursday, clicks your search ad and buys. Run only one platform and you either never reach them or lose them at the close. The accounts that scale profitably almost always run both, with roles clearly assigned and tracking that shows the full journey.

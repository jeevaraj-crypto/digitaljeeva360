The Local SEO Playbook for 2026: How to Own the Map Pack

A step-by-step local SEO guide: Google Business Profile optimisation, reviews, citations and local content — updated for how local search works in 2026.

J
JeevaFounder, Digital Jeeva360 — AI-Powered Digital Marketer & Automation Specialist

When someone searches "plumber near me" or "best dentist in [your city]", Google shows three businesses on a map before anything else. Those three positions — the map pack — capture around 44% of all clicks on the page. Everyone else shares the leftovers.

Here is the playbook we use to get clients into those three positions, updated for how local ranking actually works in 2026.

Step 1: Treat your Google Business Profile as your second homepage

Your Business Profile is now the first impression for most local customers — many will call or visit without ever seeing your website. Complete every field, then go beyond:

  • Primary category: the single biggest ranking lever. Be specific — "Emergency plumber" beats "Plumber" if that's your money service.
  • Services and descriptions: list every service with keyword-rich descriptions. These now directly influence which searches you appear for.
  • Photos weekly: profiles with regular photo activity earn measurably more calls and direction requests. Real job photos beat stock every time.
  • Q&A: seed and answer the questions customers actually ask — before a stranger answers them wrongly for you.

Step 2: Build review velocity, not just review count

Google cares about recency and consistency, not just totals. Forty reviews arriving steadily over a year outrank two hundred that arrived in one suspicious week — and reviews mentioning your services and city ("brilliant boiler repair in Leeds") act as keyword signals too.

The fix is systematic: an automated, polite request after every completed job, sent by SMS or WhatsApp within hours while satisfaction is highest. Reply to every review — positive and negative. Response quality is a visible trust signal to the next customer reading them.

Step 3: Fix your citations once, properly

Your business name, address and phone number must be identical everywhere they appear — directories, social profiles, data aggregators. Inconsistencies ("St." vs "Street", an old phone number on a forgotten listing) erode Google's confidence in your data. A one-time cleanup across the 50 or so directories that matter, then quarterly monitoring, is enough for most businesses.

Step 4: Build local landing pages that deserve to rank

If you serve multiple suburbs or towns, each meaningful area deserves its own page — not a doorway page with the city name swapped, but a genuinely useful page: local proof, local reviews, area-specific service details, directions and a map. One strong suburb page can capture hundreds of monthly searches you're currently invisible for.

Step 5: Prepare for AI-assisted local search

More local discovery now happens through AI: Google's AI Overviews summarise "best X in Y" queries, and assistants like ChatGPT recommend businesses by drawing on reviews, structured data and authoritative mentions. The winners are businesses with rich structured data (LocalBusiness schema), strong review profiles and consistent citations — the same fundamentals, with higher stakes.

The 90-day priority order

  1. Weeks 1–2: Business Profile rebuilt to best practice; LocalBusiness schema added to your site.
  2. Weeks 2–4: citation audit and cleanup; review automation switched on.
  3. Weeks 4–8: first batch of suburb/service pages published.
  4. Weeks 8–12: local link building — sponsorships, chambers, local press; second content batch.

GEO & AEO: How to Get Your Brand Cited by ChatGPT and Google AI

AI assistants now answer your customers' questions directly. Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) and Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) decide whether you're in the answer.

J
JeevaFounder, Digital Jeeva360 — AI-Powered Digital Marketer & Automation Specialist

A growing share of your customers no longer clicks ten blue links. They ask ChatGPT which agency to hire, let Google's AI Overview summarise their options, or have Perplexity compare products for them. If AI answers are the new front page, the question becomes: whose content do the machines trust enough to cite?

That's what GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) and AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) are about — and early movers are capturing outsized visibility while most businesses haven't started.

What's actually changed

Traditional SEO optimised for a ranked list; you fought for position #1. AI search engines synthesise an answer from multiple sources and cite a handful of them. Being cited — or being the recommended brand in the answer itself — is the new position #1. And the criteria for getting there overlap with classic SEO, but aren't identical.

AEO: structure your content to BE the answer

Answer engines extract and reproduce answers. Content that gets extracted shares a shape:

  • Question-first headings. Write headings the way people ask: "How much does a website cost?" — then answer in the first sentence below it, directly, before elaborating.
  • Self-contained answers. Each section should make sense quoted alone. If your answer depends on three paragraphs of setup, it can't be cited cleanly.
  • FAQ schema everywhere it's honest. Structured Q&A markup remains the clearest machine-readable signal that your page answers a specific question.
  • Facts, numbers, specifics. Generative engines preferentially cite content with concrete data and clear claims over vague marketing prose.

GEO: build the authority the models trust

Language models weigh consensus and authority across the web. Getting cited means being present in the sources models lean on:

  • Consistent entity information. Your brand name, what you do, and where you do it should be described consistently on your site, directories, LinkedIn and every profile. Models triangulate; contradictions dilute confidence.
  • Structured data. Organization, Service, Product and Review schema give models unambiguous facts to work with.
  • Third-party validation. Reviews, press mentions, industry listicles and genuine expert commentary are disproportionately influential — models treat independent sources as stronger evidence than your own website.
  • Deep, original content. Original research, real case studies with numbers, and genuinely expert explanations get cited; thin rewrites of other articles do not.

What to do this quarter

  1. Rewrite your top ten pages with question-based headings and direct first-sentence answers.
  2. Deploy FAQ, Organization and Service schema sitewide.
  3. Audit your entity consistency across every profile and directory.
  4. Publish one genuinely original asset — research, benchmark data, or a detailed case study with real numbers.
  5. Test monthly: ask ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google the questions your customers ask, and track whether you appear. What gets measured gets improved.
Classic SEO isn't dead — it's the foundation. GEO and AEO are the floors being built on top, and right now the elevator is nearly empty.
